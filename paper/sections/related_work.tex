\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Tool-augmented and memory-augmented agents.}
ReAct~\cite{yao2023react} interleaves reasoning with environment actions, and Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion} extends this with iterative self-correction.
Several systems augment LLM agents with persistent memory: Voyager~\cite{wang2023voyager} builds a verified skill library in Minecraft, GITM~\cite{zhu2023gitm} stores successful goal decompositions, Generative Agents~\cite{park2023generative} use a memory stream with reflection, MemoryBank~\cite{zhong2024memorybank} incorporates forgetting curves, and EvoSkill~\cite{alzubi2026evoskill} automatically discovers and evolves reusable skills from execution failures.
A common thread is the assumption that accumulated knowledge---whether stored as memories or skills---is net-positive.
Our work challenges this by showing that the same episodic memory mechanism yields $+19$pp gains in one environment and $-31$pp degradation in another.

\paragraph{Interactive benchmarks.}
ALFWorld~\cite{shridhar2021alfworld} provides compositional household tasks with a fixed action grammar and shared sub-goal structure, making past experience highly reusable.
WebShop~\cite{yao2022webshop} simulates e-commerce with free-form search and unique products, where successful strategies rarely transfer.
This structural contrast motivates our comparative design.

\paragraph{Retrieval-augmented generation.}
RAG~\cite{lewis2020rag} improves outputs by conditioning on retrieved documents, and has been extended to agentic settings with trajectory or demonstration retrieval.
While the RAG literature focuses on improving retrieval quality under the assumption that relevant retrieval helps, our results reveal a complementary failure mode: when retrieved experiences are \emph{superficially} similar but \emph{structurally} non-transferable, retrieval actively misleads the agent.
To our knowledge, this is the first work to systematically characterize when episodic memory helps versus hurts LLM agent performance, providing a diagnostic framework based on task compositionality, action space regularity, and learning transfer radius.
